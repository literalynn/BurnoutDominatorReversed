# Changes to PS2Recomp

Complete base kept from `ran-j/PS2Recomp`, at the commit given in `UPSTREAM.json`, under its GPLv3 license. Game files and their translation stay in directories ignored by Git.

## The VU scheduler clears its register scoreboard in place (October 25, 2026)

- `ps2xRuntime/src/lib/vu/ps2_vu1_core.cpp`: `VU1Interpreter::resetScheduler` cleared the 1 KiB ready-cycle table of the float registers with `= {}`, which MSVC built as a zeroed copy on the stack and then copied into the member. It is now a `memset` of the member. The scheduler is reset twice for every VU0 microprogram call on the game thread, about 2,200 times per VBlank in a race. Game-thread profile over the six-car race: 11 samples in `resetScheduler` against 19. The GIF stream is identical over the 12.6 million packets of the race run, and so is the RDRAM at VSync 18,000.

## The IOP interpreter's step is inline in its loop (October 25, 2026)

- `ps2xIOP/src/emulator/core/iop_cpu_execute.h` (new), `iop_cpu.cpp`, `iop_cpu.h`: `IopCpuCore::executeInstruction` and its two helpers move unchanged from `iop_cpu.cpp` into a header, as inline functions, which only `iop_emulator.cpp` includes. Called once per IOP instruction from another source file, the step saved and restored eight registers on every call.
- `ps2xIOP/src/emulator/iop_emulator.cpp`: `step` and `checkInterrupt` are forced inline as well; in the larger loop MSVC left `checkInterrupt` out of line, a call on every instruction. The instruction and cycle counters are incremented apart: MSVC added them as one 16-byte vector, and the load of the cycle counter that follows waited for its store (31 samples on that load before, 11 now).
- Game-thread profile over the six-car race: `runCpu`, which now contains the interpreter, takes 131 samples against 178 for `runCpu` and `executeInstruction` together, and the IOP 10.6% of the busy samples against 13.6%. The GIF stream is identical over the 12.6 million packets of the race run, and so is the RDRAM at VSync 18,000. The IOP and runtime tests pass, and the touched files build with GCC 13.

## FPU and VU0 macro additions, subtractions and multiplications run inline (October 25, 2026)

- `ps2xRuntime/include/ps2_float.h`: MSVC kept `ps2_fadd`, `ps2_fsub`, `ps2_fmul` and the VU0 macro forms `ps2_vadd`, `ps2_vsub` and `ps2_vmul` out of line in the generated functions. Every such operation was a call with three clamps, each a round trip through an integer register with two branches, and the vector forms saved and restored eight XMM registers. Over the six-car race they took 71 of the 2,038 busy game-thread samples, filed under `PS2Runtime::isSpecialAddress`, the public symbol just before them. On x86 they are now inline, in about nine instructions and without branches. The generated code runs them on the game thread, whose MXCSR reads denormal operands as zeros of their sign (DAZ), which is what the operand clamps did, and rounds toward zero, so that finite operands cannot overflow into an infinity. What is left is exponent 255 in the operands, brought down to +-FLT_MAX by two integer minimums, and denormal results, cleared to a zero of their sign with an integer mask (a multiplication by one would do it under DAZ, but compilers may drop it). Other targets keep the clamping versions.
- `ps2xTest/src/ps2_runtime_expansion_tests.cpp`: the float semantics test runs under the game thread's float modes, with an operand read at run time so that the compiler cannot fold the overflows in its own rounding mode. New test: under those modes the helpers give the bits of the clamping formulas for 40 special patterns (zeros, denormals, the normal limits, infinities and NaNs of both signs) against 4,040 patterns both ways, and lane by lane on vectors. A standalone run of the same comparison over 16 million pairs finds 2.1 million differences in the default modes, 131,639 with rounding toward zero alone and none with both.
- Measured on the deterministic six-car race (5 alternated runs, medians): game thread CPU 4.80 to 4.42 s, lower in every pair, and 1,945 busy samples in its profile against 2,038. The executable is 3 MB smaller (79.0 against 82.1 MB): the calls and the spills around them took more room than the inline code. The GIF stream is identical over the 12.6 million packets of the race run, and so is the RDRAM at VSync 18,000.

## The IOP interpreter keeps its step in registers and reads each instruction once (October 25, 2026)

- `ps2xIOP/src/emulator/core/iop_cpu.cpp`, `iop_cpu.h`: ADD, ADDI and SUB went through two lambdas that MSVC did not inline. Their closures captured the other lambdas by reference, so every instruction built closure objects on the stack and kept the step's locals (the written register, the scheduled load, the new branch) in memory, with a 0xA8-byte frame. The overflow test is now a plain function and the three cases write their result themselves; the frame is 0x58 bytes and the locals stay in registers. `executeInstruction` takes the instruction word from its caller.
- `ps2xIOP/src/emulator/iop_emulator.cpp`: `step` already reads the word at the PC to recognize import stubs (`jr $ra`); it now passes that word on instead of letting the interpreter read it again.
- Game-thread profile over the six-car race: `executeInstruction` 87 samples against 126, the IOP 20.0% of the busy samples against 21.9%. The GIF stream is identical over the 12.6 million packets of the race run, and so is the RDRAM at VSync 18,000.

## VIF unpacks of 32-bit V1/V2 and in offset mode take the fast path; VU routine entry and exit cost less (October 25, 2026)

- `ps2_vif1_interpreter.cpp`: counted over the six-car race, 12% of the vectors VIF1 unpacks went through the general per-element loop (a division and a mask test for every vector and field): Burnout's V3-8 in offset mode (26 million vectors, STMOD 1 adds ROW to every element) and V2-32 (5.6 million). The per-format loop for 16- and 8-bit data now also takes 32-bit elements and the offset mode: ROW is added to the four lanes after the format's own steps, as the general loop does (a V3's w lane included, read from the next element or kept). Masked unpacks, the difference mode, mode 3 and CL < WL keep the general loop. New test: V3-8 in offset mode.
- `vu/ps2_vu1_program.cpp`: `find`, which looks up the compiled routine at every microprogram start, answers from its cache without a stack frame; the search for an entry it does not hold moved to a separate function. `handBack`'s test for VF results still in flight takes the unsigned maximum of the 32 registers' ready cycles in four independent registers instead of a compare and an OR per register.
- Measured on the deterministic six-car race (5 alternated runs, medians): GS worker CPU 5.55 to 5.38 s (lower in every pair), game thread 4.88 to 4.81 s (VU0 calls go through `handBack` too). The GIF stream is identical over the 12.6 million packets of the race run, and so is the RDRAM at VSync 18,000; compiled VU routines still match the interpreter on the synthetic fixtures.

## The EE checkpoint runs without a stack frame when nothing is due (October 25, 2026)

- `Kernel/EeScheduler.cpp`, `runtime/ee_scheduler.h`: `checkpointDue` runs at every backward branch and guest dispatch. Its full path calls other functions when something is due, so MSVC gave it a stack frame and saved registers at every call. It now tests first whether nothing is due (no device batch to flush, no timer interrupt, no deadline or end of time slice reached, no pending checkpoint or stop, the running thread cached when COP0 Count runs) and then only charges the cycles, as the full path would, without a call: a leaf function without a frame. Any other checkpoint goes through the former function, renamed `checkpointDueSlow`, from its start. Game-thread profile over the six-car race: the two functions took 75 of 2,084 busy samples, against 92 of 2,109 for `checkpointDue` before. The GIF stream is identical over the 12.6 million packets of the race run, and so is the RDRAM at VSync 18,000.

## Debug switches are read at start-up (October 25, 2026)

- `Kernel/EeScheduler.cpp`: `BDR_WATCH_WORD` and `BDR_DISPATCH_HISTORY` were read into function-local statics, tested at every guest dispatch: each test was a call and a thread-safe initialization check. They are now constants initialized at start-up, as `BDR_EE_CYCLE_SCALE` already was; nothing sets the environment later. Game-thread profile over the six-car race: the two functions took 7 and 7 of 2,126 busy samples, now none.
- `vu/ps2_vu1_core.cpp`: the same for `PS2_VU_CAPTURE_DIR`, `PS2_VU_PROFILE_EXECUTION` and `PS2_VU_AOT_PROFILE`, read at every VU run. With the three changes above, over 1,500 VBlanks of the race, alternating four runs against the executable before them: game thread CPU medians 4.85 s before, 4.78 s after (runs vary by about 4%).

## IOP import calls compare library names without the C runtime (October 25, 2026)

- `iop_emulator.cpp`, `imports/iop_imports.cpp`: `dispatchImport` compares the name of a called import's library with up to twenty built-in ones, and `IopImportRegistry::findLibrary` with every export table when a call goes to a guest library such as LIBSD. Both lowered each character with `std::tolower`, a call into the C runtime. They now lower ASCII letters inline, the same mapping as `std::tolower` in the C locale, which the runtime never changes. Game-thread profile over the six-car race: `tolower` took 11 of 2,126 busy samples, now 1, and `dispatchImport` 8, now 3.

## The game thread reads denormal operands as zero (October 25, 2026)

- `Kernel/EeScheduler.cpp`: on x86 `EeScheduler::run` sets MXCSR's DAZ bit along with rounding toward zero, and puts both back on return. The EE FPU and VU0 have no denormals (`ps2_float.h`). The float helpers already flushed their operands, so their results are unchanged; comparisons and the other operations without a helper now read a denormal operand as a zero of its sign.
- `vu/ps2_vu1_core.cpp`: a VU0 call found the game thread without DAZ and switched MXCSR on entry and on return, two reads and two writes of the register for every microprogram. It now finds MXCSR as it needs it (comment updated). With the debug switches read at start-up below, `VU1Interpreter::run` and `execute` took 44 of 2,126 game-thread busy samples over the six-car race, now 13. The GIF stream is identical over the 9.7 million packets of the race run, and so is the RDRAM at VSync 18,000.

## Generated code writes a register's low half with one store (October 25, 2026)

- `ps2_runtime_macros.h`: `SET_GPR_U32`, `SET_GPR_S32`, `SET_GPR_ZE32` and `SET_GPR_U64`, used by almost every guest instruction, merged their 64-bit result into the 128-bit register: a load of the register, a blend and a 128-bit store, so each write waited for the register's previous one. `Ps2SetGprLow64` now takes the 64-bit value and stores only that, leaving the upper half as before. Game-thread profile over the six-car race: guest code took 812 of 2,390 busy samples, now 679, and the executable shrinks from 94.6 MB to 82.0 MB. With the three changes below, over 1,500 VBlanks of the race, alternating five runs against the executable before them: game thread CPU medians 5.34 s before, 4.95 s after. The GIF stream is identical over the 12.6 million packets of the race run, and so is the RDRAM at VSync 18,000.

## LQ and SQ to the scratchpad take one call (October 25, 2026)

- `ps2_runtime_macros.h`: a guest 128-bit load or store outside plain RAM goes out of line to `Ps2SlowRead128` or `Ps2SlowWrite128`. These called `PS2Runtime::Load128` or `Store128`, which serve aligned scratchpad quadwords in place; Burnout moves most of its per-object data through the scratchpad. The first function now serves the scratchpad itself, with the same test, and sends the rest to the runtime as before. Game-thread profile over the six-car race: `Load128` and `Store128` took 37 of 2,390 busy samples, now 1.
- `ps2xTest/src/ps2_runtime_kernel_tests.cpp`: a test reads and writes a quadword in the scratchpad and in the uncached RAM mirror through these macros.

## SPU2 samples are clamped without std::clamp (October 25, 2026)

- `core/iop_spu2.cpp`: `clamp16`, run for every interpolated voice sample, called `std::clamp`, whose reference result made MSVC keep the bounds in memory and select an address. It now compares values, and `decodeAdpcm` uses it instead of its own `std::clamp`. Over a randomized run of 48 voices (3.4 million output frames, rounding toward zero and to nearest), the output, ENDX, IRQ count and sound RAM hash the same as before and the run takes 8% less time. Interpolating all voices in a separate branch-free pass was slower than this change alone.

## The IOP scheduler walks its threads in a vector (October 25, 2026)

- `core/iop_kernel.h`, `core/iop_kernel.cpp`: `beginNextReady` runs at every pass of the IOP loop, about 3,100 times a VBlank in a race, and nearly always finds no thread ready. It walked the `std::map` of threads, a pointer chase per node. It now walks a vector of pointers to the map's nodes, rebuilt after any insertion or removal (a map node keeps its address until it is erased). It picks the same thread, the lowest priority then id among the ready ones, in whatever order it walks them. Game-thread profile over the six-car race: `beginNextReady` took 67 of 2,390 busy samples, now 18.

## The IOP interpreter steps without a stack cookie (October 25, 2026)

- `iop_emulator.cpp`: `step()` and `schedulePendingDma()`, called for every IOP instruction (about 33,000 a VBlank in a race), held their trace and error paths inline. Their string streams gave both functions a large frame (0x190 and 0x170 bytes, seven or eight saved registers) and a stack cookie, set up and checked at every call, and kept `step()` out of line. Those paths are now separate functions that are never inlined (`stopOutsideRam`, `traceCallsAndReturns`, `stepImport` for the `jr $ra` that may be an import stub, `startPendingDma`); the per-instruction path keeps the same tests in the same order and now inlines into `runCpu`. Over 1,500 VBlanks of the six-car race, alternating runs against the executable before this change and the previous one: game thread CPU medians 5.84 s before both, 5.45 s after both (wall unchanged, the headless run waits on GPU readbacks). The GIF stream is identical over the 12.3 million packets of the shorter run, and the RDRAM at VSync 18,000 differs only in words that also vary between runs of one executable.

## The EE checkpoint charges its cycles without nested calls (October 25, 2026)

- `Kernel/EeScheduler.cpp`, `runtime/ee_scheduler.h`: generated code reaches `EeScheduler::checkpointDue` at every backward branch and every guest dispatch, about 80,000 times a VBlank in a race. Each call went through a thread-safe static (the `BDR_EE_CYCLE_SCALE` percentage, a TLS check every time), then `accountCycles`, then `currentThread`, three frames for a few additions and compares. The percentage is now read once at start-up; `accountCycles`' work is a force-inlined private member (`chargeCycles`, which `accountCycles` also calls) whose batch flush to the EE timers and the IOP is out of line (`flushDeviceCycles`), and it tests the running thread's cache in place. Same operations in the same order. Game-thread profile over the six-car race: the checkpoint path took 6.3% of the busy samples, now 3.3%. The GIF stream is identical over the 12.3 million packets of the shorter run, and the RDRAM at VSync 18,000 differs only in words that also vary between runs of one executable.

## GsSubmit's queue keeps its jobs' data in one buffer (October 24, 2026)

- `gs/gs_async_backend.cpp`: every queued job carried a byte vector of its own and a copy of the presentation request, 160 bytes a job and a heap block whenever no recycled buffer had the room. The data of the queued jobs now lies back to back in one byte vector and the presentation requests in another; the `GsSubmit` thread swaps both with the queue, as it does the jobs, and a job keeps its data's offset and size (24 bytes). Steady state allocates nothing. Over 1,500 VBlanks of the six-car race, alternating runs of the same executables: wall 7.61, 7.55, 7.64, 7.68 s before, 7.46, 7.44, 7.44, 7.46 s after; GS worker CPU medians 5.75 and 5.58 s, `GsSubmit` 5.26 and 5.08 s. The paraLLEl-GS VRAM saved at VSync 61,000 is identical byte for byte, as between two runs of one executable.

## VU runs leave denormal operands to the host's DAZ (October 24, 2026)

- `vu/ps2_vu1_core.cpp`: on x86 `VU1Interpreter::run` sets MXCSR's DAZ bit (denormals are zero) along with the VU's rounding toward zero, and puts both back on return. SSE arithmetic then reads a denormal operand as a zero of its sign, which is what the VU does and what every FMAC path already did by hand. A denormal that one host operation produces and the next one reads inside a run, such as a square in an EFU sum or a product rounded before its addition, now also reads as zero, in the interpreter and in compiled programs alike. The game thread, which already rounds toward zero, now sets MXCSR at each VU0 call as well.
- `vu/ps2_vu1_program.h`: the SSE fast paths of compiled FMAC instructions no longer flush their operands (`flushDenormals`, four instructions an operand, one in product sums): the host does (`PS2_VU_HOST_DAZ`, x86 only; NEON and sse2neon, which also flushes results, keep the flush). A denormal operand keeps its bits, so the zero checks take it for a non-zero value and send its lanes to the exact path, with the same results. GS worker CPU over 1,500 VBlanks of the six-car race, alternating runs of the same executables: a median of 6.35 s before, 5.99 s after. The GIF stream is identical over the 12.3 million packets of the shorter run, and the RDRAM at VSync 18,000 differs only in words that also vary between runs of one executable.

## Compiled VU pairs store only the ready cycles something can read (October 24, 2026)

- `ps2_vu1_program.h`: every compiled pair stored the cycle its VF and VI results land, lane by lane, in `Run::m_vfReady` and `m_viReady`, for the later pairs that may stall on them and for the hand-back to the interpreter. Both read a lane's cycle only while it is ahead of the clock. Three more pairs in the same block take the clock past a result's landing (`kMaxWriteLatency`, 4 cycles), and past the cycle its lanes held before, which an earlier pair stored; until then only the third pair can check those lanes. A pair now skips the store (`kHiddenReadies`, computed at compile time from the block's words and the reads `settledReads` proves) when the two pairs after it cannot stall and the third does not check its lanes. The one exit inside a block, after a GIF packet rewrote microcode during PATH1 (`Frame::stale`), first stores the cycles its pair and the two before it skipped (`Frame::staleReadies`): no pair in between stalled, so each one's issue cycle follows from the clock. GS worker CPU over 1,500 VBlanks of the six-car race, alternating runs of the same executables: 6.45, 6.78, 6.67, 6.78 s before, 6.38, 6.50, 6.25, 6.31 s after (medians 6.73 and 6.34 s, 6% less); storing no ready cycle at all, which is not exact, gave 6.19 to 6.31 s. The GIF stream is identical over the 19.1 million packets of the shorter run, and the RDRAM at VSync 18,000 differs only in words that also vary between runs of one executable.
- `ps2_vu1_program_tests.cpp`: a pass where the first GIF packet turns the code into pairs that only read registers (ADD into vf0 and IADD into vi0; over six shifts, the three pairs after any point read every register), so that a compiled run leaves part-way through a block and the interpreter then waits for the results still in flight. Without `staleReadies` 7 of the 28 fixture entries end on another cycle. With MAX instead of ADD, 3 of them end with other MAC and status values, before this change too: an FMAC result a routine does not queue (`kFlagsUnseen`) relies on a later result of the routine to replace it, which code rewritten in the meantime does not have.

## paraLLEl-GS precompiles the pipelines its renderer requests (October 24, 2026)

- `cmake/ParallelGSPatches.cmake` (`gs_renderer.cpp`): at start-up paraLLEl-GS compiles its shader variants on half the CPU's threads (`kick_compilation_tasks`). The driver keeps them, so this costs only on a first run or after a driver update, and NVIDIA's cache is per executable name: then it took 542 CPU seconds over 74 s here (8 threads of a Ryzen 7 9800X3D), while the game boots. The list now holds the keys the renderer requests. A pass shades its super samples and then its single samples with the subgroup size range of its rate (`dispatch_shading_commands`), so each rate is compiled with its range, for its own constants and for zeros; upstream compiled every rate for [16, 64], [8, 64] and [4, 64] invocations, which on GPUs without wave64 are three keys for the same pipelines and leave out the [1, 64] and [2, 64] keys of passes at one and two samples a pixel. Single-sampled variants have the super-sample reference bit when VRAM has room for samples (`dispatch_shading` always sets it then), also with a texture array; the uploads' fourth constant (an indirection buffer or not) is set, which the list left out; only the rates `set_super_sampling_rate` can set are compiled (ordered or rotated grid, up to the supported maximum), and each key once. The copy, upload and binning shaders come first, then the configured rate and single sampling, and the threads take turns through the list: each had a slice, and the last `num_tasks % threads` tasks were left out (`hardware_concurrency()` returning 0 divided by zero). Boot without a state under a new executable name: 364 CPU seconds over 55 s. The GS thread still compiles the variants the first frames need (20 synchronous compiles at the start of the boot, black screen, against 32 there before); later runs are unchanged (0.7 CPU seconds of compilation, none synchronous).

## The compiled VU1 routines can be built by clang-cl (October 24, 2026)

- `CMakeLists.txt`: with MSVC, when Visual Studio's clang-cl (component *C++ Clang Compiler for Windows*, in `VC/Tools/Llvm/x64/bin`) is installed, it compiles the generated VU1 routines (`vu_programs_N.cpp`), the eight shards in parallel (policy CMP0147 for those commands only), and their objects go into `ps2_runtime` instead of MSVC's. Release options as for the rest (`/O2 /arch:AVX2 /GS- /Gw`), except `/fp:precise` and `-ffp-contract=off`: no FMA where MSVC has none. `PS2X_VU_PROGRAM_CLANG_CL` names another clang-cl (recent enough for the STL: clang 20 for Visual Studio 2026's), `OFF` keeps MSVC. Six-car race replayed from one state, 1,500 VBlanks, built with clang 19: GS worker 6.23 s median against 6.56 s with MSVC (alternating runs, the same executables warm); the GIF stream is identical over the 7.9 million packets of the MSVC run, and the RDRAM at VBlank 18,000 differs only in words that also vary between runs of one executable (kernel stacks, SPU2 timing).

## The paraLLEl-GS backend follows GIF data without reading every quadword (October 24, 2026)

- `gs/gs_parallel_backend.cpp`: `observeGIF`, which follows the GIF stream for the transfer registers (`BITBLTBUF`, `TRXREG`, `TRXDIR`) that `GetTransferSnapshot` reports, stepped through the data one quadword at a time. Only PACKED A+D can write those registers, so PACKED data without A+D and REGLIST data (two registers a quadword, the last half unused after an odd count) are now passed in one step, to the end of the tag or of the packet: 1.1% of the GPU submission thread in a race at 240 frames a second instead of 4.3%. `ps2xTest/src/ps2_gs_tests.cpp`: a transfer set up behind both kinds of data is still followed.

## With vsync the window waits for the compositor (October 24, 2026)

- `ps2_runtime.cpp`, `runtime/host_sleep.h` and `host_sleep.cpp` (new `waitForComposition`: `DwmFlush` on Windows, nothing elsewhere): with vsync, NVIDIA's OpenGL swap waits for the monitor when the monitor still has a frame to show, and it waits holding a lock that the GS backend's Vulkan calls also take (`vkAllocateMemory`, `vkBeginCommandBuffer`). The game's frames waited for the monitor with it: at `refresh_rate = 144` on a 60 Hz screen a race ran 62 VBlanks a second, in slow motion, and at 60 with 16 samples a pixel and the high-resolution scan-out it ran 56.2 and 56.5 race steps a second instead of 60 (58.9 with one sample a pixel). With vsync the window loop now waits for the desktop compositor's next frame after each draw, so a swap finds the monitor free and returns at once: 144 VBlanks and race steps a second on the 60 Hz screen, and 60 race steps a second at 60 with any number of samples (the status lines, printed by the window loop, now come about 8 ms late and read 145 and 60.5). Without vsync nothing changes.

## Scan-out and texture images come from Granite's heaps (October 24, 2026)

- `cmake/ParallelGSPatches.cmake` (`gs_renderer.cpp`): paraLLEl-GS creates its scan-out images (the circuits, the merged image and the deinterlaced one) at every vsync. They are colour attachments, which NVIDIA's driver prefers in allocations of their own, so each one was a `vkAllocateMemory` and a `vkFreeMemory`, 18 MiB at 4x resolution with the high-resolution scan-out: at 240 frames a second they took about an eighth of the GPU submission thread (`NtGdiDdDDICreateAllocation`, `NtGdiDdDDIDestroyAllocation2`). They now have `IMAGE_MISC_FORCE_NO_DEDICATED_BIT`, and Granite places them in its 64 MiB heaps, which it keeps. Window race at 240 frames a second with 16 samples a pixel, vsync off, same executable with and without (an environment switch while measuring), alternating runs: 196.1 and 195.5 VBlanks a second against 190.3 and 190.9 (the game runs 240 steps a second either way, so more of them are drawn); the headless deterministic race at 240 frames a second with one sample a pixel runs 217.3 (216.6 before).
- The texture cache's images (`create_cached_texture`) too: paraLLEl-GS keeps them for reuse only at power-of-two sizes up to 1024, and the others came and went with allocations of their own, another 5% of the GPU submission thread at 240 frames a second. Same race and switch: 197.4 and 197.8 VBlanks a second against 193.1 and 193.2; frames dumped from the window during a race show the same pictures.

## Frames stay on the GPU on Windows (October 24, 2026)

- `gs/gs_parallel_backend.cpp`, new `runtime/shared_frames.h` and `shared_frames.cpp`: on Windows, `Present` copies the scan-out into one of four Vulkan images that the window's OpenGL context imports (`GL_EXT_memory_object_win32`, `GL_EXT_semaphore_win32`), instead of reading it back to host memory for the window to upload again: 18 MiB a frame each way at 16 samples a pixel with the high-resolution scan-out. Each image is released to OpenGL in the shader-read layout, with an exported semaphore that OpenGL waits for before drawing it; the window gives a slot back once an OpenGL fence placed after its last draw has passed (it checks them while it waits for frames too), and a frame the window has not taken yet is replaced by the next one, whose copy waits for the replaced one's. When no slot is free the frame is read back as before; readbacks older than the last shared frame are dropped, so such a frame is never an old one. `Present` still waits for the previous frame's copy, as it waited for its readback. Frames are shared only when the window's OpenGL context has the extensions, runs on the Vulkan device (same LUID) and is NVIDIA's, the only driver tested (`BDR_SHARED_FRAMES=1` tries others); with the CPU rasterizer, in headless runs, after an OpenGL or Vulkan error and with `BDR_SHARED_FRAMES=0`, they go through host memory as before. A line of the log says which.
- `gs/gs_frontend.cpp`: `GS::present` takes a frame with a size as a frame, since a shared frame has no pixels; the CPU and asynchronous backends return frames without a size when they have none.
- `ps2_runtime.cpp`: the window draws the shared frame's texture, FXAA included; F9 screenshots and frame dumps read that texture back when no pixels are latched. Between frames the window loop gives back the slots OpenGL has finished drawing.
- Same window race at 144 frames a second with 16 samples a pixel and the high-resolution scan-out, vsync off, same executable, alternating runs: 144.1 and 144.0 VBlanks a second (the game's rate, lowest second 144) against 137.3 and 134.1 (lowest 131 and 122) through host memory; at 240 frames a second, 190.0 against 156.1, the GPU 85% busy against 81%. At 60 frames a second with vsync the race runs 60 VBlanks a second either way, and the headless deterministic race at 240 frames a second with one sample a pixel is unchanged (216.6). Window captures and frame dumps show the same pictures. Counted in these runs: no frame found every slot taken, and at most 9 frames in 12,000 were replaced before the window took them (at 240 frames a second).

## The GPU submission thread no longer waits for the GPU in the middle of frames (October 24, 2026)

## Lighter checks in compiled VU1 arithmetic (October 24, 2026)

At 240 frames a second the GS worker is busy nearly all the time, three quarters of it in the compiled VU1 programs. By instruction kind, about 40% of their samples fall on the fast FMAC path's denormal flushes and range checks, against 1% on the arithmetic itself. Each change below leaves the GIF stream of the deterministic race as it was (9 to 15 million packets compared) and the compiled-against-interpreter test passing with the fixture programs, whose operands include denormals, infinities, NaN, 1e-30, 1e30 and 3.4e38. - `ps2_vu1_program.h`: a compiled MADD, MSUB or OPMSUB (and their ACC forms) flushed both operands with `flushDenormals`, took their exponents again for the range checks, and built the whole check (zero product, risky product, zero result) at every instruction. The exponents are now taken once from the operands as loaded: an exponent of 0 is a zero or a denormal, so the same compare flushes the operand. A quick test first accepts the lanes whose result is well inside the range with no product far above it; such a lane passes the whole check too and is not zero, so the whole check is only built when a lane fails the quick one. `fast::all<Dest>` names the movemask test the checks share. 7.59, 7.67, 7.55, 7.78 s before, 7.41, 7.58, 7.48, 7.44 s after.
- `ps2_vu1_program.h`: an operand written earlier in the same block by an FMAC instruction, ABS or ITOF, in every lane the instruction reads, holds no denormal, infinity or NaN, since those results never do. `normalOperandsOf` finds these operands for each pair at compile time (registers are unknown at the block's start, vf0 aside; a lower write to the register the upper half also writes is dropped, as `issue` drops it) and `Frame::pair` passes them as hints, `kLeftNormal` and `kRightNormal`, that skip the flush. 7.55, 7.52, 7.56, 7.48 s before, 7.41, 7.42, 7.41, 7.55 s after.
- `ps2_vu1_program.h`: each range bound took two compares and an AND. An add first carries the values past the upper bound beyond INT32_MAX, so one signed compare checks both bounds (FLT_MIN < |result| < FLT_MAX for ADD, SUB and MUL). For the products the quick test accepts result exponents from 2 to 233 with a product exponent at most 146 above the result's, a subset of what the whole check accepts, and the whole check moves into the branch the quick test rarely takes. 7.45, 7.42, 7.55, 7.41 s before, 7.31, 7.34, 7.31, 7.33 s after.
- `ps2_vu1_program.h`: an upper instruction's result went into a `float[4]` whose address `fast::exactLanes` takes, so MSVC stored it on the stack and read it back before writing the VF register or ACC, a store-forwarding delay in every chain of dependent instructions. ## VU0 random numbers (October 24, 2026)

- `vu_translation_helpers.cpp` (the game code is generated again): the VU0 macro instructions on the R register did not do what the PS2 does. VRNEXT stepped an invented 128-bit generator and never wrote its destination, so the game kept whatever its VF register held. VRINIT and VRXOR filled R from other made-up generators. 55 game functions draw random numbers this way. The crash debris take their size from the eighth power of one: with stale registers the sizes reached 1e10 to FLT_MAX, and the debris became huge flat polygons drawn over the sky for a few seconds after each crash. That was the flashing, recoloured sky (FINDINGS E020). R now works as in the VU0 micro mode interpreter: a 23-bit LFSR with taps at bits 4 and 22, read as a float in [1, 2) and held in every lane of `vu0_r`. VRINIT keeps the low 23 bits of its operand and VRXOR XORs them in. VRNEXT steps R and writes it to its destination fields; with vf0 as destination it still steps R but writes nothing. In the deterministic replay of a crash at Glacier Falls, the debris sizes are 0.06 to 0.35 and the sky stays normal. Test: `code_generator_tests.cpp`.

## Steady race speed with keep_speed (October 24, 2026)

- `EeScheduler.cpp`, `runtime/ee_count.h` (new), `ps2_save_state.cpp`: with `keep_speed`, COP0 Count added the host time the game thread waited for the GS worker to the EE cycles. That time was often counted twice: when the wait ended before the next VBlank, the VBlank schedule had not lost it, so emulated time had not stood still. ## The game keeps its speed when drawing falls behind (October 19, 2026)

- `EeScheduler.cpp`: COP0 Count was never advanced, so Burnout's frame governor (0x1E6728, called by 0x209338 with 1 to 4 steps, 2 to 8 in its 30 Hz mode) always ran one simulation step per drawn frame and the race slowed down with the frame rate. ## MR32 stalls only on the lanes it reads (October 15, 2026)

- `ps2_vu1_exec.inl` (`decodeLowerUsage`): MR32 was treated as reading all four lanes of fs, so it waited for any pending write to fs. It reads the rotated lanes (destination x reads fs.y, ..., w reads fs.x), as PCSX2's `_vuRegsMR32` does. Burnout Dominator's car reflection loop (car microcode 0xC20-0xD90) is scheduled to the cycle: its two `MFP` at the loop top expect `ERSQRT` then `ESQRT` results one cycle apart. The false stall on `MR32.w vf22, vf19` after `MR32.z vf19, vf15` delayed the loop by one cycle, so one vertex in four read the next `ESQRT` result, its sphere-map coordinates left the reflection disc, and the car paint showed red and green patches. Interpreted and compiled VU programs share this decoder. Test: `MR32 waits only for the source lanes it rotates in`.
- `ps2_vu1_tests.cpp`, `VU capture replay`: prints the cycle count, VI1 and P, and `BDR_VU_REPLAY_OUT=file` saves VU data memory at the end.

## sceMcGetDir: negative count, directory names and search mode (October 15, 2026)

- `Stubs/MemoryCard.cpp`: MCSERV forwards a negative entry count to MCMAN with no table (`MCSERV.IRX`, GETDIR handler at 0xAD0), and MCMAN (0x80FC) then counts the matching entries without writing any. The runtime clamped a negative count to 0 and returned 0, so after a race Burnout Dominator found no save directory and asked for the original memory card forever. A path without wildcards also matched the directory's contents instead of the directory itself (MCMAN matches the last path component in its parent directory): the game's free-space walk then changed into `.`, `..` and the save files. The mode argument was ignored: a non-zero mode continues the previous search after the entries already returned (MCMAN keeps a cursor), and the game reads its save directory one entry at a time with it, so it got the first entry forever and stayed on "Checking memory card" after a race. The cursor is not part of save states. Tests: `sceMcGetDir with a negative count returns the entry count without a table`, `sceMcGetDir on a directory name returns that directory's entry`, `sceMcGetDir with a non-zero mode continues the previous search`.

## PATH3 queue read in place (October 15, 2026)

- `ps2_memory.cpp`, `ps2_memory.h` (`m_path3FifoHead`): each MSKPATH3 window concatenated the whole masked PATH3 queue to find its GIF packets and queued the remainder again, so a frame with a hundred windows over a 300 KiB texture queue copied tens of MiB. The queue is now read in place from a head offset and only released bytes are copied (about 8% more VSyncs per second in the race, identical RDRAM after 1,100 VSyncs). Save states store the queue without the consumed bytes, so their format is unchanged.

## DMA chains are no longer cut at 4096 tags (October 15, 2026)

- `ps2_memory.cpp`: a source chain stopped after 4096 DMA tags and the rest was dropped as if the chain had ended. Burnout Dominator's VIF1 display list passes 4096 tags at high speed in the race, so the end of the frame (texture uploads included) was lost and every other frame showed the track with wrong textures and colours. The DMAC has no such limit; the guard against a chain that loops forever is now 2^20 tags or 64 MiB of data, and reaching it is reported on stderr.

## PATH3 packets released by a window go out before the DIRECT that follows (October 15, 2026)

- `ps2_memory.cpp` (`releaseMaskedPath3Packets`): the PATH3 packets an unmask window lets out were queued in the GIF arbiter without draining it, so a VIF1 DIRECT later in the same window was sorted ahead of them by path priority. A PATH3 packet the GIF has started ends before PATH2 is served (continuous mode); the released packets now go out at once. Burnout Dominator draws each piece of its front-end images as `MSKPATH3 0`, texture upload on PATH3, `DIRECT` draw, `MSKPATH3 1`: the first piece was drawn before its texture arrived, so the 50/60 Hz screen showed stale VRAM and the title logo read "RNORNOUT OMINAOMINATOR".
- `BDR_TRACE_DMA_KICKS=first,count`: GIF, VIF0 and VIF1 DMA starts with the queued sizes and the PATH3 mask.

## VIF0 UNPACK shares the VIF1 decoder; V4-5 scaling (October 15, 2026)

- `ps2_vif1_interpreter.cpp`: VIF0 UNPACK only handled 32-bit formats and ignored masking, ROW/COL, STMOD and the S/V2/V3 layouts, so 16-bit, 8-bit and V4-5 data never reached VU0 memory. ## SQRT.S reads ft; VF0 in every context (October 15, 2026)

- `fpu_translator.cpp`: the R5900 encodes `SQRT.S fd, ft` with the operand in the ft field and fs = 0 (unlike MIPS IV, PCSX2 `SQRT_S`); the translation read fs, so every square root in the game returned sqrt(f0). In Burnout Dominator all 778 SQRT.S read f0: vector normalizations failed, car orientation matrices were built from unnormalized rotations (rows of length 0.02 to 0.2, the up row their cross product, so the car bodies were squashed flat and only wheels and lights showed) and distances fed to the camera were wrong. ## VU0 microprograms run to their end (October 15, 2026)

- `PS2Runtime::executeVU0Microprogram` (`ps2_runtime.cpp`): a VCALLMS program ran at most 4096 VU cycles and was then dropped half done, its partial registers copied back to the EE. The EE waits for VU0 until the program ends, so the program now runs to its E bit, with a cap of 2^24 cycles that only stops a program that never ends (reported on stderr). Burnout Dominator's VU0 programs all end within 4096 cycles; programs with longer loops were cut short.

## EE floating point: no Inf, NaN or denormals (October 15, 2026)

- `ps2xRuntime/include/ps2_float.h` (new): the EE FPU and the VUs have no infinities, NaNs or denormals. An exponent of 255 is an ordinary value, overflow saturates to +-FLT_MAX, denormals are zero, a zero divisor gives +-FLT_MAX (0/0 included) and square roots take the absolute value (PCSX2 `FPU.cpp` and VU clamping). `FPU_ADD_S`, `FPU_SUB_S`, `FPU_MUL_S`, `FPU_DIV_S`, `FPU_SQRT_S` and the COP2 macro `PS2_VADD`/`PS2_VSUB`/`PS2_VMUL` now follow these rules; host IEEE arithmetic produced Inf, then NaN (Inf * 0), which spread through game state. In Burnout Dominator the post-processing strength became NaN during the race, a strip count derived from it was 0, and the `do { n--; } while (n)` loop of 0x1ba7f8 wrote GIF data past the scratchpad into the timer callback table, so the next timer interrupt jumped to 0x80000000.
- `fpu_translator.cpp`: DIV.S sets D/SD (I/SI for 0/0) instead of a non-EE bit and returns +-FLT_MAX; RSQRT.S computes `fs / sqrt(ft)` instead of `1 / sqrt(fs)`.
- `vu_translation_helpers.cpp`: VDIV returned 0 for a zero divisor, VSQRT 0 for a negative input, and VRSQRT ignored its numerator (`Q = 1 / sqrt(ft)` instead of `fs / sqrt(ft)`); they now use `ps2_fdiv`, `ps2_fsqrt`, `ps2_frsqrt`.
- Rounding stays round-to-nearest; the EE rounds toward zero. Test: `EE float semantics: no Inf, NaN or denormals`.
- `EeScheduler.cpp`: `BDR_WATCH_WORD` reports also print the GPRs; `BDR_WATCH_DUMP=file` saves EE RAM at the first report.

## MMI semantics (October 15, 2026)

An audit of the MMI translation against the EE Core Instruction Set Manual and PCSX2's interpreter (`pcsx2/MMI.cpp`) found most HI/LO and permutation instructions wrong: the parallel multiplies and divides (PMULTW, PMULTUW, PMADDW, PMSUBW, PMADDUW, PDIVW, PDIVUW, PDIVBW, PMULTH, PMADDH, PMSUBH, PHMADH, PHMSBH) summed every product into one scalar instead of filling the 128-bit HI/LO pair; PMFHI, PMFLO, PMTHI, PMTLO, PMFHL and PMTHL moved 32 or 64 bits instead of 128 (HI1/LO1 are `hi1`/`lo1`); PEXEH, PEXEW, PROT3W, PREVH, PEXCH, PEXCW, PINTH and PINTEH used wrong element orders; PSLLVW, PSRLVW and PSRAVW shifted rs by rt on four lanes instead of words 0 and 2 of rt by rs with 64-bit sign extension; PADDUH and PSUBUH did not saturate; PSLLH, PSRLH and PSRAH used five bits of sa instead of four.

- `ps2xRuntime/include/ps2_mmi.h` (new, included by `ps2_runtime_macros.h`): reference implementations of these instructions on `R5900Context`.
- `ps2xRecomp/src/lib/mmi_translation_helpers.cpp`, `mmi_translator.cpp`: emit calls to them; the wrong macros (`PS2_PINTH`, `PS2_PEXEW`, `PS2_PMFHL_*`, `_mm_custom_sllv_epi32`...) are removed from `ps2_runtime_macros.h`.
- Burnout Dominator only uses PABSW, PSLLH and PSRAH of these, so its behaviour changes through the PABSW fix below; the rest matters for other games. Test: `MMI instructions use the 128-bit HI/LO pair and EE element orders`.
- `EeScheduler.cpp`: `BDR_RDRAM_DUMP=vsync,file` saves EE RAM at that VSync tick.

## Checkpoint unwinding and interrupt masking (October 14, 2026)

- `ps2xRuntime/include/runtime/ee_unwind.h` (new), `ps2_runtime.cpp`, `EeScheduler.cpp`: when a checkpoint asks generated code to return to the scheduler, a thread-local flag is set and `dispatchGuestBranch` returns false after any call, instead of comparing `ctx->pc` with the callee's entry. That comparison (meant for handlers that leave `pc` unchanged) mistook an unwinding mutual recursion for a finished call: in Burnout, 0x336458 calls 0x335D88, which calls 0x336458, which calls 0x335D88 again; a checkpoint on that innermost call left `pc` = 0x335D88, the outer call to 0x335D88 was taken as finished, and 0x336458 went on with the inner frames still on the stack and read its return address from the wrong slot. This is the crash of BOOT.md §15 (jumps to 0xB415CB, 0x1, 0x17A, 0x11B...): 3 runs out of 4 under load before, 0 out of 12 after. The scheduler clears the flag before each dispatch; an unneeded unwind only costs a resume through the return address's entry. Test: `dispatchGuestBranch call keeps unwinding when a checkpoint stops at the callee entry` (455/455).
- `EeScheduler.cpp`: queued handlers (INTC, DMAC, alarms, VSync and SIF callbacks) start only when the running thread has EIE set, i.e. not between the game's `di` and `ei`, as on the EE.
- `EeScheduler.cpp`: `BDR_DISPATCH_HISTORY=1` keeps the last 256 scheduler dispatches (thread, pc, sp, ra) and prints them when the scheduler reaches a pc without generated code.

## GS queue and debugging aids (October 14, 2026)

- `gs_cpu_backend.h/.cpp`: CLUT loads, host-to-local and local-to-local transfers, image uploads, TEXFLUSH, framebuffer clears and single-pixel VRAM writes no longer make the submitting thread wait for the raster workers. They are queued with the primitives as serial operations: when the workers reach one, the last to arrive runs it (`std::barrier` completion) while the others wait, so it sees every earlier operation finished and none of the later ones started. A primitive sampling a range written by a queued primitive now queues such a barrier instead of draining. FINISH no longer waits (the game only learns the GS is done; every host read of VRAM still waits). Local-to-host transfers, VRAM reads, snapshots, presentation and reset still drain. The backend destructor drains the queue, so the caller's VRAM holds everything submitted. `GetTransferSnapshot` reports the last requested transfer while workers run. With 8 workers on the intro video: from 25 to 31 VSync/s, game thread waiting on the GS from 42% to about 15–25% of the time.
- `ps2xTest/src/ps2_gs_tests.cpp`: a scene that uploads a 32-bit texture, samples it, overwrites it, samples it again, then loads an 8-bit texture and its CLUT twice (TEX0 with CLD = 1) between draws must give byte-identical VRAM with 4 workers and with immediate drawing. 454/454 runtime tests pass.
- `ps2_ipu.cpp`: the output FIFO is a byte vector with a read index instead of a `std::deque<uint8_t>` filled and emptied one byte at a time.
- `EeScheduler.cpp`: `BDR_DETERMINISTIC=1` lets VBlank, alarms and timers fire on EE cycles alone instead of also waiting for the host clock; idle time is skipped and runs repeat exactly (the guest cycle count at a given VSync is identical from run to run). Game speed is then unrelated to real time. `BDR_WATCH_WORD=0xADDR[:0xVALUE]` checks a guest word after every dispatched function and prints the last 32 dispatched functions when it changes (or becomes VALUE).

## Performance (October 13, 2026)

- `ps2xRuntime/include/runtime/ps2_wait_stats.h` (new), `EeScheduler.cpp`, `gs_cpu_backend.cpp`, `ps2_runtime.cpp`: each `[status]` line reports the share of wall time the game thread spent waiting since the previous one, by cause (`idle`: no EE thread ready; `pace`: an event due in EE cycles waits for host time; `gs`: waiting for the GS raster workers; `gslock`: GS backend held by the presentation thread), and the VSync rate. Owner's profile in the menus: 41% of the game thread's samples in a Windows wait, cause unknown until now.
- `EeScheduler.cpp`: `BDR_EE_CYCLE_SCALE=P` charges P% of the cycles reported at checkpoints (default 100, unchanged behaviour).
- Measured on the CPU rasterizer (one thread, 640x448 sprite): 20 ns per pixel untextured, 43 textured, 62 textured and blended, 134 bilinear.

- `ps2xRuntime/src/lib/gs/gs_cpu_backend.cpp` and `gs_cpu_backend.h`: deferred, multi-threaded CPU rasterization. `Submit` queues primitives; worker threads (`BDR_GS_THREADS`, default half the cores, at most 8; 0 or 1 keeps the former immediate drawing) draw them in interleaved 8-row bands, so each pixel is written by one thread in submission order. Every operation that reads or writes VRAM or the CLUT (VRAM access, transfers, CLUT load, clear, TEXFLUSH, FINISH, presentation, snapshots, reset) waits for the queue first. - `ps2xTest/src/ps2_gs_tests.cpp`: a scene of blended Gouraud triangles with depth test and a render-to-texture sprite sampled without TEXFLUSH must give byte-identical VRAM with 4 workers and with immediate drawing. `main.cpp` keeps the other GS tests, which read raw VRAM right after a draw, on immediate drawing. A 20,000-triangle scene gives the same VRAM hash with and without the row spans. ThreadSanitizer: 453/453 tests, no report.
- `ee_scheduler.h`, `EeScheduler.cpp`: the running thread is cached instead of looked up in the thread map after every guest dispatch (about 8% of the EE thread in a cloud profile); the cache is cleared on every erase. The dispatch loop tests the generated function table inline, and `dispatchGuestBranch` looks a target up once instead of three times.
